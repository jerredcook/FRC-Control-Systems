Robot Roles

Pick a job.
Play it well.

A match is three robots a side, and the best alliances hand out jobs before the buzzer: scorer, feeder, defender. Today you are the coach doing the handing.

The alliance

One great robot is not a plan

Your alliance is three robots. The scoreboard does not care which one of them scores.

Part 1 taught you to read the game: the clock, the rules, the scoring math, the standings. Part 2 is about playing it, and playing starts with a question every alliance answers before every match: what job should each robot do? Think of a pace line on a group ride. One rider pulls at the front and breaks the wind, one sits behind and saves legs for the sprint, one watches the road and calls the turns. Same bikes, different jobs, and the group is faster than any rider alone.

Scouts sort ORBIT robots into four rough archetypes. An archetype is not a rulebook thing, it is a performance envelope: how fast the robot cycles, which ring it feeds, and where it ends the match. Only three fit on the floor, so every match, one job gets benched.

Why it matters

Roles are chosen, not welded on

Here is the part that surprises new team members: a role is a per-match decision, not your robot's identity. Your build sets which jobs you can do well, the same way your bike's gearing sets which hills you can climb, but the lineup around you decides which job pays most tonight. A robot that scored all morning might spend the afternoon feeding a faster partner, and the team that switches without sulking is the team every captain wants.

Defense deserves one honest warning before you fall in love with the flip. It works by crowding a rival, and crowding is exactly where the pinning rules from Lesson 02 live. Every foul hands the other alliance points, so a careless Defender slowly turns into a scoring machine for the wrong side. Play defense with a stopwatch in your head.

The quiet job wins picks

At a real event, alliance captains remember the robot that took the boring job and did it perfectly. A Feeder that never missed a handoff, a Defender that never drew a foul. When the draft room opens in the next lesson, watch how much that reputation is worth.

Going further · optional

Real FRC scouting runs on the same two moves you just practiced: stats like OPR estimate each robot's scoring envelope, and playoff second picks are very often defenders, chosen to erase the other alliance's star.
