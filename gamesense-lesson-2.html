Lesson 02 Read the Game
Reading the Manual

Read it like
a referee

Nobody memorizes the manual. Good teams learn to find the right rule fast - and today you make five referee calls yourself.

Every match has two scoreboards.

There is the one where you earn points, and a quieter one underneath where you give them away. In ORBIT a penalty never subtracts from your score. The referee adds points to the other alliance: for a foul, for a tech foul.

foul → + to them  ·  tech foul → + to them There is no minus sign on your side. A penalty is a gift, delivered straight to your opponents.

Do the comparison and it gets uncomfortable fast. A tech foul hands over points, and a high-ring - the best single score in the game during teleop - earns . One careless bump in the wrong zone cancels a whole trip to the Reactor by your best scorer.

Which makes the manual the cheapest points in the sport: every penalty you do not commit is scoring you never had to do. This lesson is about how to actually read the thing.

Anatomy

Every rule has the same skeleton

Rules look like dense legal text until you see the bones. Here is a real one from the ORBIT manual:

O101

Read it again slowly and it breaks into four parts: a name (O101), an act (holding a second ), a number ( seconds), and a price (FOUL). Every rule in every manual you will ever read has this shape.

The name

A short ID you can say fast. Refs and drive coaches talk in IDs, so one number gets everyone staring at the same sentence.

The number

Almost every rule has something countable: seconds to drop an extra , meters to reset a pin. Refs count. They do not vibe.

The price

FOUL (+) or TECH FOUL (+). The class tells you how much the game cares about what just happened.

Navigation

Know the neighborhoods

Nobody carries a whole manual in their head, and referees do not either. What they carry is the map: which family of rules lives where. In ORBIT's Section O, the first digit does the sorting for you:

O1xx ·

Rules about game pieces: how many you may hold, where they may fly.

O2xx · Contact

Robot on robot: pushing, pinning, and the places contact is forbidden.

O3xx · Endgame

Protections that switch on in the final seconds, when points are biggest.

It is like your first day at a big bike park. You do not memorize every sign on every trail. You learn the map at the trailhead - green loops here, jump lines there - and then read the one sign that matters when you actually reach it.

So when something happens on the field, you do not start reading at page one. You ask which family is this, jump to that neighborhood, and read one or two sentences carefully. That is the whole skill, and the drill below trains exactly it.

This transfers to the real game

Real FRC manuals pull the same trick with letters: G rules govern gameplay, R rules govern how the robot may be built. The numbers reshuffle every January when a new game drops, but navigating by family never goes stale. Learn it once on ORBIT, use it every season.

Try it

The scavenger drill

Here is all of Section O - the complete penalty section of the ORBIT manual, five rules. Beside it, five moments from a match, and you are the referee. For each one, find the rule that decides it and tap it, then make the call: legal, foul, or tech foul. The manual stays open the whole time. Real refs look things up mid-match, and so should you.

Predict first
Partway through this drill, Red 1 pins a Blue robot for seconds, backs off meters, then comes back and pins for more - seconds of pinning in total. What should the referee call?
O204 runs each pin on its own clock, and separating by meters resets it. Each pin here stayed under the -second limit, so the referee keeps the whistle down. This exact moment is one of the five scenarios - find the reset clause in the manual panel and watch your gut argue with it.

Going further · optional

In real FRC the manual is a living document. FIRST publishes Team Updates during the season that change rules mid-year, and runs an official Q&A where any team can ask what a rule really means - the answers are binding at every event. Strong teams give one student the job of reading every update the day it drops and reporting the changes at the next meeting. It is one of the highest-value jobs on a team, and it needs no code and no shop. Try this: which of ORBIT's five rules would you most want to ask a clarifying question about, and what exactly would you ask?

Show one good question

Here is one for O101: "The rule says a robot may control at most one Cell. If a second Cell gets trapped against our bumper while we drive, are we controlling it?" That is the real shape of a good Q&A question. It takes one word from the rule ("control"), finds its fuzzy edge, and asks about a situation your robot will genuinely be in. The best questions come from picturing your robot doing its normal job and asking where the rule's words get blurry.

Checkpoint

Quick check

Pick an answer to see why.

Question 1
Your robot commits a foul. What actually happens on the scoreboard?
Penalties are a gift to your opponents: for a foul, for a tech foul. The final margin works out the same as losing points yourself, but knowing where they land helps you read a scoreboard while the match is running.
Question 2
Which of these hands the other alliance more points?
O207 is a TECH FOUL at points, while O101 is a plain foul at . The manual prices rules by how badly the act can damage the other alliance's game, and protected zones sit near the top of that list.
Question 3 · From the sim
In the drill, an opponent shoved a robot that was touching its own platform with seconds left. What did rule O301 make the referee do?
O301 protects the endgame: inside the final seconds you may not touch a robot on its own platform. The price is a tech foul ( points) plus the robot is scored as Balanced ( points) - points swinging the wrong way from one shove.
Question 4
Your robot intentionally lobs 3 over the Reactor's high ring guard. What does O115 cost you?
O115 reads "FOUL per Cell," so 3 means 3 fouls: points. Per-instance wording is exactly the kind of detail that scavenger reading catches and vibes miss.

Lesson 02 · Recap

FoulA penalty that adds points to the other alliance's score.
Tech foulThe heavier class: points to them, for acts that can wreck an opponent's match.
Rule IDThe short name (like O204) everyone uses to point at the same rule fast.
ThresholdThe countable number inside a rule: seconds held, meters backed off.
Protected zoneA place or moment where contact rules tighten, like the loading bay or the endgame platform.
Per-instanceWhen a rule charges once per piece or per event, not once per decision.
