Review a teammate PR

Someone on your team just opened a pull request. Before it merges, a human reads it - and today that human is you. Reviewing is not about catching people out. It is the friendliest quality gate your team has, and it is a skill you can practice.

Why bother

A review is a gift, not a gotcha

Every line of robot code gets read far more often than it gets written.

A review is the moment a second pair of eyes catches the small stuff before it turns into a mystery at competition - a motor that overheats, a command that fights itself, a number nobody can explain at 11pm the night before a match. Catching it now, kindly, is a gift to your teammate and to future-you.

A good review comment does three things: it points at one specific line, it explains why it matters, and it names the fix - never the person. "You always forget this" stings and teaches nothing. "Add addRequirements here so the scheduler knows who owns the intake" actually helps.

Values with no name

A bare number sitting in the code that only its author understands. It wants to be a named constant.

Safety that vanished

Current limits, soft limits, brake modes that quietly disappeared in a diff. Ask why before you approve.

Commands with no owner

A command that drives a motor but never claims its subsystem. A missing requirement lets two commands grab the same mechanism.

The other half of review

Say it kindly

Finding the problem is only half the job. The comment you write is the part your teammate actually reads. Aim for specific and warm: point at the line, explain the risk, offer the fix, and assume they are smart and busy, not careless. You want them to open your review and feel helped, not judged.

Question 1
You spotted the missing addRequirements. Which review comment is the most helpful?
The best comment points at one line, explains why it matters, and names the fix - warmly. It treats the miss as normal (it is) and hands over the solution instead of the blame.
Question 2
The PR deletes the roller's SupplyCurrentLimit. Why flag it?
Supply current limits protect the breaker and the wiring, and stator limits protect the motor. A stalled intake pulls the most current of anything, so removing that cap risks a brownout mid-match. Ask why it was deleted, and loop in a mentor before dropping it.
Question 3
You find a bare 0.85 buried in execute(). Best move as a reviewer?
A magic number is not a crash, it is a maintenance trap. Suggesting a named constant makes the intent obvious and gives one place to tune. Suggest it kindly and let your teammate make the edit.
🤝 On your team · this week

Next pull request your team opens, leave one comment that follows the recipe: point at a line, say why it matters, offer the fix. If you are not sure something is a real problem - a deleted current limit, a soft limit that changed - ask a question instead of a demand, and check with your mentor before anyone merges a change to a safety config. A review that asks "was this on purpose?" is worth ten that say "this is wrong."

Going further · optional

Great reviews are easier when PRs are small. A change that touches 30 lines gets a careful read; one that touches 800 gets a tired "looks fine." When it is your turn to open a PR, keep it focused and write a one-line summary of what changed and why - you are making it easy for the next reviewer to be kind and thorough. The deeper habit: treat code as something the whole team reads together, not something one person owns alone. New to command requirements or current limits? The glossary has both.
