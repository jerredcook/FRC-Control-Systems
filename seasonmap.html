The shape of a season

Six weeks and a buzzer

FRC runs on a clock, and the clock is short.

On one Saturday in early January, thousands of teams watch the same video and learn the same new game at the same instant. That is kickoff. From there you get roughly six weeks to design, build, program, and tune a robot, and then you take it to a competition. Everyone gets the same six weeks. The teams that do well are not the ones who work the most frantic hours, they are the ones who spend those weeks in the right order.

Think of it like a group bike trip with a hard departure time. If you spend all morning arguing about the route, you never actually ride. If you ride off before anyone reads the map, you end up somewhere useless, fast. The winning move is a quick, honest plan first, then steady work, then a little practice before you go.

This season The practice game - ORBIT

Everything on this map points at one short match. In ORBIT, a match lasts just 150 seconds: 15 of autonomous, 135 of driver control, and the last 30 for the endgame scramble onto the Platform. A single high-ring Cells scores 8 points, and balancing on the Platform is worth 20. Every week below is really about being ready for those 150 seconds.

Why the order

Each week earns the next

Notice how the map only works forward. You cannot tune a controller in week 5 until the robot is integrated in week 4, and you cannot integrate until there are real parts from fabrication and real code from simulation. You cannot fabricate the right parts until a prototype proved the idea, and you cannot prototype the right idea until kickoff told you what the game rewards.

That is the single most useful thing this map teaches: strategy is not the fun part you rush past to get to building. It is the decision that every later week inherits. Get it right on day one and the other forty days point the same direction.

The two weeks everyone underbudgets

Tuning and driver practice sit at the end, so when anything upstream slips, they are what gets cut. A robot that lurches to its target and a driver who has never run a real cycle will lose to a plainer robot that is smooth and well-driven. Protect weeks 5 and 6 the way you would protect the finish line of a ride, not the part you sprint through, the part you planned the whole route to reach.

Going further · optional

The classic "six-week build season then bag the robot" is a useful model, but the real rules have loosened. FIRST removed the stop-build "bag day" back in 2020, so teams can now keep improving the robot between competitions instead of freezing it. Many teams also play a district schedule with several events across a few weeks, which turns the season into a loop: compete, learn what broke, fix and re-tune, compete again. The map below still holds - the order of the work is the same - it just repeats. And once the season ends, the best teams keep the loop going in the off-season, which is the calmest time to actually learn everything in this academy.

Checkpoint

Quick check

Pick an answer to see why.

Question 1
When does a team decide what its robot should actually do?
Strategy is a kickoff decision. You read the manual, do the scoring math, and pick what your robot must do (and refuse to do) before anyone touches a tool. Every later week inherits that choice, which is why Game Sense sits first on the map.
Question 2
Why do software teams write and test code in the WPILib simulator during weeks 2-3, before the robot even exists?
Simulation lets the code crew work in parallel with the build crew, like practicing a bike route on a map before you ride it. The same program that ran in sim runs on the robot at integration, so week 4 is not starting from scratch.
Question 3
Your team is running behind. Which phases do teams most often crush down to almost nothing, and regret?
Delays roll downhill and land on weeks 5 and 6. Tuning and driver practice are the first things sacrificed and the last things you can afford to lose, which is why protecting them is a plan you make at kickoff, not a hope you have in week 6.
Question 4
What has to be true before week 5 tuning can even begin?
Tuning is making a working robot good. That means the hardware and code must already meet and behave at integration in week 4. You cannot smooth out a controller for a mechanism that is not wired up and moving yet.
