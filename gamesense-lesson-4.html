Ranking Points

Lose the match,
climb the table.

Wins move you up the standings, but they are not the only thing that does. Meet the ranking point, the quiet number that decides who picks playoff partners first.

The standings

One long weekend, one big table

A tournament is not one match. It is a stack of them.

Through the qualification rounds, every team plays the same number of matches, each time with a different random pair of partners. All weekend, one big table quietly sorts every team at the event: the standings. Your row in that table is your seed, and it matters more than any single match, because when qualifications end, the top seeds take turns choosing their playoff partners. Seed high and you pick. Seed low and you hope to be picked.

What moves you up the table is not wins. It is ranking points, RP for short. In ORBIT, a win pays RP, a tie pays , and a loss pays . So far that sounds like every sport you know: win days, collect points, lead the series.

Here is where it stops sounding like every sport you know.

The twist

Points for doing it right

Game designers want robots practicing the hard skills all season, not just squeaking out wins. So they attach ranking points to accomplishments, things your alliance does during a match that pay off in the standings whether you win or lose. ORBIT has two:

Read the fine print twice: the bonuses do not care who won. Score at least Reactor points and is yours, even in a loss. Get all three robots docked or balanced and is yours, even in a loss.

Do the math

A loss that pays like a win

match RP = result + Energized + Harmony best possible match: win + both bonuses = RP · worst: plain loss = RP

Now do the arithmetic that surprises almost everyone at their first event. Lose a match but grab both bonuses: + + = RP. Win a match with no bonuses: RP. The same number. In the standings, that loss and that win are identical.

Think of a bike club's summer series. You get points for finishing near the front, sure, but the series also hands out points for riding every hill climb and for the whole crew finishing together. A rider who never wins a single Saturday can still lead the series by stacking up the steady points that the sprinters skip. RP standings work exactly like that, and teams that only chase wins get passed by teams that chase points.

Try it

You keep the standings

Six made-up teams, four qualification matches each, and you are the scorekeeper. Pick a team, set each match to win, tie, or loss, and flip the two bonus toggles. The table re-sorts the instant anything changes. Break the standings, fix them, and see how far bonuses alone can carry a team.

Why it matters

The last seconds change

Once you see the standings this way, matches look different. A team cruising to a comfortable win should stop scoring and go balance, because winning by more pays nothing extra while follows them into the standings. And a team getting soundly beaten is not out of things to play for: the bonuses are still on the table, and RP from a lost match is the same pay as a plain win. Good teams sprint hardest when they are losing.

Fewer wins, higher seed

At a real event it is normal for the 2 seed to have more wins than the 1 seed. Nobody cheated. One team treated bonus RP like homework and turned it in every single match. If your sim standings ever look strange, read the bonus column before you read the win column.

Going further · optional

Two extras. First: real FRC standings rank by the average RP per match, called the ranking score, so a team that plays one fewer match is not punished. ORBIT uses totals because all six teams play the same four matches, which gives the same order with simpler math. Second, a strategy puzzle: it is the endgame, your alliance is losing by a lot and a comeback is not happening. You sit Reactor points short of the line at , and all three robots can reach the platform. What do you do with the time?

Show the thinking

Send your fastest scorer for one more high-ring Cell. It is worth exactly points, which lands you on the threshold, and "at least" means exactly enough counts. Meanwhile the other two robots head home and dock. But notice the trap: needs all three robots docked or balanced, so the scorer's trip back matters as much as the Cell. If the clock only allows one bonus, take the one you can actually finish. Either way you leave a lost match carrying real RP, which is the whole reason this puzzle has a right answer.
