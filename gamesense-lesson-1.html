Anatomy of a Match

One match,
three clocks

Before you can outscore anyone, you have to know when the points happen. One scripted ORBIT match, one scoreboard, and a scrubber to take it apart second by second.

Welcome to Game Sense.

This course is about the half of robotics that needs no wrench and no laptop: reading the game, doing the math, and making the plan. Every lesson runs on ORBIT, our practice game. Two alliances of three robots collect , feed the Reactor tower at center field, then hurry home to settle on their platform before the buzzer. We wrote the rules ourselves, so they never go out of date.

Here is the first thing to know about any FRC-style match: it is not one block of time. It is three stretches with three different personalities. Think of a bike race with a strange start: for the first stretch your bike rides a route you programmed the night before, hands off. Then you grab the handlebars and ride the long middle. And in the final sprint, the finish line itself pays prize money.

s auto + s teleop + s endgame = s One catch: endgame is not a new period. It is the final s of teleop, split out here because it pays differently.

Auto

For seconds the robot is on its own, running moves your team wrote ahead of time. Nobody touches a controller.

Teleop

Drivers take over for the long middle. Score, feed partners, play defense - this is where most of the driving happens.

Endgame

The final seconds of teleop. Nothing pauses, but the platform awards switch on and the smart money heads home.

The price list

Auto pays double

ORBIT scores on three rings of the Reactor tower, and each ring has two prices. Score during teleop and you get the normal rate. Score the exact same during auto and it pays double. Why? Because scoring with no human on the controls is genuinely hard - the game designers pay extra for it, the way a harder trick earns a higher score.

Auto has one more prize, and it is the cheapest in the game: simply driving fully out of your start zone earns points, called Leave. Free points for any robot that can move in a straight line. Good teams never skip it.

Rungs upgrade, they do not stack

A robot that docks and then levels the platform is scored as Balanced, not Dock plus Balance. The ladder pays your best rung once. Keep that in mind when you watch the scoreboard in a moment - you will see a Dock turn into a Balance.

What it means

Reading the staircase

That rose line is the most important shape in this whole course. Points arrive in events - a lands, a robot balances - and between events the score just sits there while robots drive back and forth. So a match is really a staircase: the height of each step is the value of the thing you did, and the flat stretch before it is the time it cost you.

Once you see the staircase, strategy stops being vibes and becomes two honest questions. Can we make our steps taller, by scoring a better ring or grabbing the platform? Can we make our flats shorter, by driving cleaner routes? Everything a scout writes down and every plan a drive coach makes is an answer to one of those two questions.

And notice what the phase counters told you. The endgame window is the shortest stretch of the match, yet in our scripted match it out-earned the whole middle of teleop, mostly on the strength of one . That is not a fluke of our script - it is how the game is priced. A team that treats the last seconds as an afterthought is leaving a stack of points on the field.

Say it like a scout

When you watch any match, real or ORBIT, practice calling the score in phases: so many in auto, so many in teleop, so many in the endgame. Three little numbers tell you more about a robot than the final score ever will - and in a few lessons you will use exactly that habit to build a pick list.

Going further · optional

The platform has one more trick: the Harmony bonus. When two or more alliance robots balance together, each balanced robot earns an extra points on top of its -point Balance. Here is a puzzle worth doing with a pencil: if all three robots on an alliance balance together, what is the alliance's total endgame haul? Work it out before you peek.

Show the thinking

Each of the three robots earns a full Balance plus the Harmony bonus, so the alliance banks points in the final seconds alone - more than our scripted robot earned in its entire match. That is why you will hear drive coaches shouting for everyone to come home with time still on the clock: a half-finished cycle is worth nothing, but a third robot on the platform changes the match. Real FRC games do the same thing with their own endgame bonuses, and the teams that win in October are the ones that did this arithmetic in March.
